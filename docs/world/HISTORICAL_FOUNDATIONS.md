## 2. Architectural Traditions & Building Materials

Himalayan vernacular architecture reflects centuries of seismic resilience and climate adaptation:
- **Timber Post-and-Beam Skeleton:** Heavy Sal timber frames joined with precision mortise-and-tenon joints, allowing buildings to flex during earth tremors without structural collapse.
- **Sun-Dried & Kiln-Fired Bricks:** Deep red terracotta masonry bound with mud mortar.
- **Carved Wooden Overhangs:** Broad, sloping roofs projecting 1.5 metres beyond exterior walls, shielding delicate mud plaster from torrential monsoon rains.
- **Jhingati Tile & Thatch Roofs:** Interlocking terracotta curved roof tiles on permanent civic halls; thick mountain thatch on pastoral huts.

## 3. Social Cohesion: Guthi & Dhunge Dhara

1. **The Guthi Trust System:** An ancient socio-religious cooperative framework where community members pool labor, land, and resources to build public works (canals, bridges, rest-houses, retaining walls) and support bereaved families.
2. **Dhunge Dhara (Communal Stone Spouts):** Masterworks of medieval hydraulic engineering, delivering continuous cool, natural subterranean spring water through elaborately carved stone spouts.
